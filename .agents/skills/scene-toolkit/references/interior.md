# Interior build order

Use shell → apertures → camera → large furnishings → visible fittings → materials → lighting → spatial review. Reconstruct what the image supports; complete hidden connections plausibly and mark them inferred.

## Geometry

Model wall/floor/ceiling thickness, reveals and frame profiles. Do not use a glass plane to imply a physically absent opening. Check finished floor elevations, skirting and threshold transitions. For stairs, create a slab opening and verify headroom in section; placing visible steps below an uncut slab is a common hero-view illusion.

Furniture needs credible dimensions and assembly: support frame, cushion volumes, seams, feet, tabletops and legs. Use licensed assets when they match better than a procedural approximation, recording source and substitutions. Upholstery should not consist solely of intersecting chamfer boxes when closeups demand folds and contact compression.

## Spatial review

Inspect door swings, clear walking routes, curtain stacks, furniture-to-glazing gaps and contact shadows. Clearances depend on project requirements; do not claim accessibility/code compliance from a generic preset. Drawings and the applicable local requirements govern construction decisions.

## Materials and lighting

Match grain direction to construction, assign different fabric behavior to curtains and cushions, and retain glass reflection while showing room depth. Visible downlights, coves and lamps should correspond to actual emitters. An unseen fill light may help art direction, but record it rather than implying an exact physically measured reconstruction.

Deliver a hero view plus one plan/section or secondary view showing the room remains coherent. The toolkit provides workflows and simple plan geometry; it does not contain an automatic full furniture reconstruction model.
